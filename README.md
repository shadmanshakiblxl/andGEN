# &GEN 
> **Advanced Biological Intelligence Applications**

&GEN is a portable, AI-driven biological research assistant and clinical bioinformatics platform designed to bridge the gap between macroscopic field biology, microscopic cellular analysis, and precision genomics.

---

##  Overview & Modules

### 1. &GEN SENTINEL (Field Biology "Tricorder")
An application that acts as an all-in-one field laboratory. 
* **Detection & Scanning:** Points a camera (or microscope) at any biological entity, utilizing image segmentation and AR overlays for instant taxonomy and anatomy mapping.
* **Sentinel Safety:** Assesses biochemical and molecular hazards, flagging pathogens, venoms, or toxins in real-time.
* **Deep Analysis:** Provides comprehensive data across Taxonomy, Morphology, Molecular Biology, Biochemistry, and Comparative Relational Analysis.

### 2. &GEN Genomic Mapper ("Variant Impact Explorer")
A module tailored for **clinical genetics and oncology**, translating raw DNA variants into multi-layered 3D biological insights.
* **01 - Variant Identification & Classification:** Evaluates sequence consequences (e.g., *TP53 c.818G>A*), translating DNA changes to protein level (*p.Arg273His*) with automated pathogenicity verdicts using clinical tools (AlphaMissense, CADD, REVEL, ClinVar).
* **02 - Regulation (TF Binding):** Predicts disruptions in transcription factor binding affinity and sequence-specific DNA interactions.
* **03 - Splicing (RNA Processing):** Computes exon-intron junction architectures, donor/acceptor site deltas, and splice-site probability profiles.
* **04 - 3D Protein Structure:** Renders interactive 3D protein models (PDB data) with biophysical metrics including Fold Stability ($\Delta\Delta\gamma$), lost hydrogen bonds, and steric clash indices.

---

 Core Philosophy
* **Total Context:** Delivers full structural, evolutionary, and functional insights rather than simple labels.
* **Democratizing Science:** Consolidates multiple bioinformatics tools into an accessible, high-performance interface.
